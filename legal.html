2. What Mars Atlas is, and what it is not

Mars Atlas is an educational planning aid. It lets you draw routes on public Mars imagery, read slopes and heights from a limited elevation model, and compare options.

  • It is not a navigation system and must never be used for real rover, aircraft, spacecraft or astronaut operations.
  • Results are calculations and illustrations. They are not safety approvals, medical advice or engineering certification.
  • It is an independent project. It is not made, certified, endorsed or approved by NASA, USGS, ESA or any other agency. Their names are used only to credit their public data.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 15 November 2026. Plain-language template for an independent student project. Have it reviewed by a qualified person before relying on it legally, and fill in the details in square brackets.

Summary

  • You can use the planner as a guest, without an account. Nothing you draw is saved: it stays in the page and is gone when you reload or close it.
  • Sign in and your work is saved automatically to your account.
  • We do not use advertising, tracking or analytics.
  • If you create an account, we store the data you give us so the cloud library, sharing and chat can work.

Who is responsible

InfotronX Horizon, an independent student team. Contact: mario2008toth@gmail.ro.

What happens without an account

As a guest, your missions, routes and notes exist only in the open page and are not saved or sent to us. The page warns you before you close it with unsaved work. Small interface choices (for example that you have seen the tour) are kept in your browser. See the Cookies and local storage page.

What the site loads from other services

When you open Mars Atlas your browser contacts these services, which can see your IP address and browser details. We do not control them.

  • NASA Mars Trek (trek.nasa.gov): map imagery tiles.
  • Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com): the typefaces.
  • jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net) and esm.sh: the 3D library and the account library.
  • Our hosting provider (Netlify or Vercel): serves the site and keeps standard server logs.

If you create an account

We use Supabase to provide sign-in and storage. Depending on what you use, we store:

  • your email address and sign-in details;
  • the missions you save to the cloud, with their routes, waypoints and notes, and who you share them with;
  • an optional Crew Profile: callsign, mission role, interests, biography and an optional profile image. Roles are what you type; they are not agency credentials;
  • team chat messages in shared missions, and reports or blocks you make.

Access is limited with row-level security, so you and the people you share with can see a mission and others cannot. Staff can access data only to run, secure and support the service.

Optional AI features

The Mission Copilot has a local mode that sends nothing. An AI explanation is only requested when you ask for it, and it sends the mission details needed for that answer to an AI service run by our server. Do not include personal data in mission notes.

Why we use data

To run the planner and the cloud library, to keep the service secure, and to answer your messages. Where the law requires a legal basis, it is our legitimate interest in running the service, and your consent for optional features.

How long we keep it

Cloud data stays until you delete it or delete your account. Server logs are kept by the hosting provider for a short period.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR) you can ask to see, correct, export or delete your data, object to its use, and complain to your data protection authority (in Romania, ANSPDCP: https://www.dataprotection.ro). Write to mario2008toth@gmail.ro. You can delete local data any time by clearing your browser's site data.

Children

Mars Atlas is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their data.

Security

We use HTTPS, row-level security and do not store secrets in the code. No service is perfectly secure.

Changes

We will update this page and its date when something changes.

Cookies and local storage

Last updated: 15 November 2026.

Short version

Mars Atlas does not set cookies and does not use advertising or analytics trackers. It keeps a few choices in your browser's local storage, which is needed for the site to remember your layout and, for signed-in users, an offline copy of their missions. Because this is strictly necessary and does not track you, we do not show a cookie banner.

What is stored

KeyWhat it keepsWhy
mars-atlas.missions.v1A copy of the signed-in user's missions, tagged with their account. Removed when they sign out. Guests never get thisOffline copy for a signed-in user
mars-guest-session (session storage)That you chose to continue as a guestDo not ask again until you close the tab
mars-atlas.workflow-stepThe last step you used (Explore, Plan, Analyze, All tools)Return to where you were
mars-panel-left, mars-panel-rightWhether a side panel is hiddenRemember your layout
mars-tutorial-seenThat you have seen the tourDo not repeat it
mars-home-skipYour choice to skip the welcome screenRespect your choice
Supabase session (starts with sb-)Your sign-in session, only if you sign inKeep you signed in

None of these are sent to us unless you sign in and save a mission to the cloud.

Other services you contact

Loading the site also contacts NASA Mars Trek, Google Fonts, jsDelivr and esm.sh. They may log your IP address. See the Privacy Policy.

Control

You can clear these any time in your browser (site data / local storage). Clearing deletes missions that were saved only on this device, so export a backup first from Mission Center.

If we ever add analytics or other optional storage, we will ask for your consent first and list it here.
